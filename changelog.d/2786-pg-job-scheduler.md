### Added (2026-11-14 — #2786: Postgres job scheduler with lease and fencing token)
- **New module `aether/resource/scheduler-pg`**: a PostgreSQL-backed one-time job scheduler. Any number of instances poll one database; each job row is its own lease. Firing is AT-LEAST-ONCE: a crash or pause after the side effect and before completion re-fires the job after the lease expires, so handlers must be idempotent (the token in `FiredJob` fences their own writes).
- Claim: one `UPDATE` over `SELECT ... FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED`, token raised under the row lock; completion is `DELETE ... WHERE fence_token = ?`, so a superseded holder's completion is refused. [verified: aether/resource/scheduler-pg/src/test/java/org/pragmatica/aether/resource/scheduler/pg/PgSchedulerTest.java]
- Every time decision (due, lease expiry, retry) uses the database `now()`; local clock skew changes nothing. Residual: a database clock step moves every expiry together. [verified: PgSchedulerClockTest clockAheadInstance_doesNotFireFutureJobsOrStealLiveLease, clockBehindInstance_stillFiresDueJob]
- A handler unresolved after its lease is abandoned locally and its job retried (due after `retryDelay`); polling does not wait for handlers, at most `batchSize` firings run per instance, so one hung or slow handler delays no other job. [verified: PgSchedulerLivenessTest]
- Not provided: lease renewal, recurring jobs, per-name routing, resource-SPI wiring (follow-up work). Missed fires: a past-dated job fires once at the next poll, oldest first.
