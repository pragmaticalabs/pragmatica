### Fixed (2026-11-09 — #2981: a planned ownership move to a node outside the live ISR could lose an acknowledged record)
- **A planned move could hand a stream partition to a node outside its in-sync replica set.** With the committed owner still live, the leader's ownership writer moved the partition to its desired owner without checking ISR membership. The deposed owner keeps acknowledging on the ISR of its own view until the move reaches it, and that ISR did not contain the new owner, so under apply skew the new owner could activate without a record the deposed owner acknowledged just after (found by the TLA+ model of the stream protocol, invariant `AckedNeverLost`; not reproduced at runtime).
- A planned move now goes only to a desired owner in the live ISR, as failover already elects; otherwise the owner keeps the partition until its ISR monitor admits the desired node, and that committed ISR change re-runs the reconcile `[mechanism: IsrOwnershipWriter.successor → plannedMoveAllowed; pinned by aether/aether-stream/src/test/java/org/pragmatica/aether/stream/replication/IsrOwnershipWriterTest.java ($PlannedMove)]`. Every owner change is therefore a member of the deposed owner's ISR, which must confirm each acknowledgement and refuses the older owner epoch once it applies the change `[mechanism: all-member ISR acknowledgement over the maximal ISR + owner-epoch fence]`. The multi-node trigger cannot be forced in Ember yet; `aether/ember/src/test/java/org/pragmatica/aether/ember/EmberPlannedMoveOutsideIsrTripwireTest.java` pins why, with the decisive test disabled until two test-only seams (test infrastructure for the runtime proof of this election rule, not fencing) exist `[design intent — unverified at runtime]`.
- **Entity arcs use the same writer.** An entity ownership arc's planned move therefore follows the same rule, and only the OWNER's liveness is narrowed to the keyspace's committed hosts, so an owner that stays a live member but no longer hosts the slice counts as not live as an owner: the arc fails over, preferring a live ISR member that hosts it and otherwise any live ISR member, instead of being stranded on a node that cannot serve it or refused while an ISR member survives. A live replica that does not host keeps its ISR membership and is not fenced `[mechanism: EntityOwnershipReconciler.hostingOnly → IsrInputs.hostingMembers read in IsrOwnershipWriter.successor; pinned by aether/node/src/test/java/org/pragmatica/aether/node/EntityOwnershipReconcilerTest.java ($HostedLiveness, $NonHostIsrMember), aether/aether-stream/src/test/java/org/pragmatica/aether/stream/replication/IsrOwnershipWriterTest.java ($HostingView) and the wiring pin in LivePlacementMembersWiringTest]`. Stream writers keep the unnarrowed view and the initial ISR is never narrowed.
- Limits: a routine HRW rebalance of an entity arc to a host outside the partition's replica placement (never in its ISR) waits for as long as the owner hosts. When no live ISR member hosts the slice, a live non-host owner keeps the arc until its ISR monitor admits a host, then hands over; a dead owner fails over to any live ISR member. Nothing fences the deposed owner itself, and the closure assumes the maximal ISR covers every committed ISR member: #2993 (an `IsrMonitor` expansion still in flight is forgotten by the next tick) is a known gap there. Direction B, an ownership check at acknowledgement time on the deposed owner, is the mechanism that closes N2 regardless; it is a separate later change. The `OwnerActivation` claim "confirmation_factor >= 2 closes it" is corrected: the closure rests on this election rule, not on the confirmation factor.
