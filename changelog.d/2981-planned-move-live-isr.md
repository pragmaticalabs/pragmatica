### Fixed (2026-11-09 — #2981: a planned ownership move to a node outside the live ISR could lose an acknowledged record)
- **A planned move could hand a stream partition to a node outside its in-sync replica set.** With the committed owner still live, the leader's ownership writer moved the partition to its desired owner without checking ISR membership. The deposed owner keeps acknowledging on the ISR of its own view until the move reaches it, and that ISR did not contain the new owner, so under apply skew the new owner could activate without a record the deposed owner acknowledged just after (found by the TLA+ model of the stream protocol, invariant `AckedNeverLost`; not reproduced at runtime).
- A planned move now goes only to a desired owner in the live ISR, as failover already elects; otherwise the owner keeps the partition until its ISR monitor admits the desired node, and that committed ISR change re-runs the reconcile `[mechanism: IsrOwnershipWriter.successor → plannedMoveAllowed; pinned by aether/aether-stream/src/test/java/org/pragmatica/aether/stream/replication/IsrOwnershipWriterTest.java ($PlannedMove)]`. Every owner change is therefore a member of the deposed owner's ISR, which must confirm each acknowledgement and refuses the older owner epoch once it applies the change `[mechanism: all-member ISR acknowledgement over the maximal ISR + owner-epoch fence]`. The multi-node trigger cannot be forced in Ember yet; `aether/ember/src/test/java/org/pragmatica/aether/ember/EmberPlannedMoveOutsideIsrTripwireTest.java` pins why, with the decisive test disabled until the seams exist `[design intent — unverified at runtime]`.
- Limits: an entity arc whose desired owner (HRW over the keyspace's hosts) is outside the partition's replica placement is never admitted to the ISR and stays with its live owner. Nothing fences the deposed owner itself (#2981 direction 2). The `OwnerActivation` claim "confirmation_factor >= 2 closes it" is corrected: the closure rests on this election rule, not on the confirmation factor.
