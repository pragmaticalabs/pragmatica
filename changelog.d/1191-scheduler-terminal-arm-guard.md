### Fixed (2026-09-15 — #1191: a certificate retry timer could outlive `stop()`, leaving a live tick on a stopped scheduler)
- **Hooks run after and outside the CAS, so two transition WINNERS can overtake each other.** `Fsm.tryAdvance` returns early when the compare-and-swap fails — `onExit`, the transition action and `onEntry` all sit behind that early return — so a LOSING transition runs no hooks at all, and the class documentation says exactly that. The real interleaving is between winners: a thread that has won `Renewing → RetryBackoff` can still sit between the CAS and its `onEntry` while a second thread wins `RetryBackoff → Stopped` and completes the drain; the first then arms a retry timer onto a stopped scheduler, uncancelled and still firing its `Tick`. The CAS serialises STATE CHANGES, never the hooks that follow it. [mechanism: `Fsm.tryAdvance` returns before `onExit`/action/`onEntry` when the CAS fails; the hooks execute outside the atomic]
- **An earlier revision of this change recorded the OPPOSITE mechanism and was wrong.** It claimed a losing transition "has already begun running its side effects" and attributed the arm to the loser. An adversarial reviewer read `tryAdvance` and refuted it. The error mattered beyond wording: a wrong mechanism aims the FSM-wide follow-up at making losers skip side effects, which they already do.
- **Arming, draining and marking terminal now take ONE lock** (`Context.timerLock`). `terminated` and `scheduledTask` are separate references, so no arrangement of atomics makes "is this scheduler terminal" and "what is armed" a single observation. The check-then-store it replaces left a window in both directions: a concurrent arm could orphan the future it displaced, and the holder could be observably non-empty after `stop()` had returned — which is precisely what the original flaky assertion reads. [mechanism: one critical section over the pair; `markTerminatedAndDrain` marks and drains together rather than as two ordered steps]
- **Arming now cancels any future it DISPLACES.** The store was a bare `set` while the drain used `getAndSet`, so a second arm orphaned the first — invisible to every later drain and still firing after the scheduler stopped. Found by adversarial review, not by the author.
- **All three arming sites route through the guard** — `Healthy.onEntry`, `RetryBackoff.onEntry`, and the dev-mode-only `configureShortValidity`. Guarding one of three would have read as complete while leaving two open.
- **Three mutations produce three DIFFERENT single-test red sets, none a superset of another.** Measured over the whole test class, never over a selected method:

  | mutation | red set |
  |---|---|
  | strip the terminal check in `armScheduledTask` | `armScheduledTask_afterStop_refusesAndCancelsTheTask` |
  | drain before marking terminal | `stopEntry_raisesTerminatedBeforeDraining_soNoArmCanSlipBetweenThem` |
  | bare `set` instead of displace-and-cancel | `armScheduledTask_whenAnotherArmDisplacesIt_cancelsTheDisplacedFuture` |

  Unmutated: the class is 6 tests, 0 failures; module `integrations/net/tcp` is **70 `<testcase>` elements across its surefire reports in one run, 0 failures**, against 67 before this change. [mechanism: per-mutation red sets measured over the whole class; counts are `<testcase>` elements in that module's surefire reports, stated with their space because a count without one is not checkable]
- **The scope is one scheduler, and #1191 stays OPEN.** Its general fix is an FSM-wide design decision. One correction to the survey an earlier revision of this fragment recorded: `LeaderElectionState` is **not** in the same position — it overrides `onCasLost` specifically to cancel its eagerly-scheduled tick, a mitigation this scheduler lacked. `ClusterDeploymentState` does arm inside `onEntry` and was not examined here. [design intent — unverified]
- **Pinning the ordering needed the right entry path, and the first attempt measured the wrong drain.** `transitionTo` runs the FROM state's `onExit` before the target's `onEntry`, and `Healthy.onExit` already drains the holder — so a probe installed after `start()` is cancelled by the EXIT hook while `terminated` is still false, and that version of the test failed against correct code. Entering the terminal state from `Idle`, which declares no exit hook, leaves the terminal drain as the only one that can reach the probe. The test records what `terminated` held at the instant the drain cancelled a stub future, turning an ordering into a value observable with no concurrency and no timing.
- **What this does NOT establish, recorded beside the claims rather than left to be noticed.** The natural race #1191 reports at roughly 6% was never reproduced locally: 200 isolated runs of `stopAfterImmediateRenewal_clearsScheduledTask` and 50 whole-class runs, zero failures. This change is justified by reading the FSM and by pinned invariants, not by a before-and-after flake measurement, and no claim is made here that the CI flake is now impossible. [design intent — unverified]
