### Fixed (2026-10-25 — #1038: disabling auto-heal suppressed the replacement but not the deletion)
- **With auto-heal disabled the cluster deleted the VM of a node it had lost anyway**, taking the journal, JVM output and cloud-init logs needed to diagnose the loss. The gate now sits at the one place every confirmed-reap chain passes, first attempt and every retry (the departed-node reap, the orphan sweep, the retry of a refused or failed reap, the surplus-trim and join-grace drain reaps), and at the periodic re-check of an unconfirmed termination. While auto-heal is disabled none of them reaches the provider `terminate` call. Only a retirement an operator or the node-replacement protocol directed is not held. Auto-heal enabled: behaviour unchanged. [verified: aether/aether-deployment/src/test/java/org/pragmatica/aether/deployment/cluster/ClusterTopologyManagerAutoHealDisabledReapTest.java]
- **A held reap is now visible**: the operator event `instance-reap-held` (WARNING, with the log line) names the node and the path that was held, once per node; enabling auto-heal again, or the node joining again, raises the recovery `instance-reap-released`. The reap is parked, so the next SWIM FAULTY for the node, the activation replay or the periodic orphan sweep takes it up once auto-heal is enabled. The recovery is raised by the leader that held the reap: a release under another leader leaves the held event open. [verified: ClusterTopologyManagerAutoHealDisabledReapTest, OperatorWarningCodeTest]
