### Fixed (2026-11-03 — #2628: blueprint publish then delete could leave an orphan slice target with running slices)
- **The slice-target write of a blueprint depended on commit order.** Rabia commits pending batches in content-hash order, and the leader submitted the target put (on the blueprint's put notification) and the target removal (on its removal notification) fire-and-forget. A put that committed after the removal re-added the target for a deleted blueprint and the leader LOADed it; nothing swept it, and a leader change re-adopted it (D4). Two publishes of one blueprint id could commit their target puts in either order, and the older one could win, leaving the target diverged from the committed blueprint (D6).
- The target puts of a blueprint are now one leader transaction fenced on the exact `AppBlueprintValue` they derive from (its `attemptId` is the generation) and on the target values they were built over; the removal of a deleted blueprint's targets is fenced on the blueprint being absent. A put of a deleted or superseded blueprint is refused by the applier whatever the commit order, a removal never removes the target of a blueprint published again, and a write refused because a target moved (an autoscaler write) is rebuilt against the moved target, at most five times. The resubmission of a lost target put (#1109) takes the same fence. It does not depend on an engine ordering guarantee (#2629). [verified: aether/aether-deployment/src/test/java/org/pragmatica/aether/deployment/cluster/fsm/SliceTargetOrderingTest.java]
- A new leader no longer re-adopts a committed target whose owning blueprint is not committed: it sweeps it with the same fenced removal. [verified: SliceTargetOrderingTest$LeaderChange]
- Not covered here: the other writers of `SliceTargetKey` (autoscaler, A/B tests, the scale API, the rollback manager's compare-and-set) still write the target they read without a blueprint fence; the leader-change sweep above removes an orphan any of them leaves.
