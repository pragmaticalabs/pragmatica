### Fixed (2026-09-19 — #1240: durable entity recovery read only the local ring, so a partition whose checkpoint lagged further than the ring reaches refused service although its history was sealed in storage)
- **`StreamEntityLogSubstrate.read` served only the ring, and `earliestRetainedOffset` reported only the ring's earliest offset.** This broke the substrate interface's own contract item 3, "reads span tiers". The entity ring holds 10,000 records, or fewer when records are large. Once a partition's checkpoint fell further behind than that, the fold's gap check refused every read and write on the partition until an operator stepped in, while the missing history sat in segments this node had sealed itself.
- **Reads below the ring's earliest offset now come from this node's sealed segments** through the node's `TieredStreamReader`. Each sealed read is capped at the ring's earliest offset, so the two tiers never overlap. The records returned must sit at exactly `from, from + 1, …`, and a skipped or repeated offset is refused, never folded. If the ring evicts an offset mid-read, the read falls back to storage. `EntityFold` is unchanged. [mechanism: `SealedHistoryRecoveryTest`, which drives the real `EntityFold` against the real substrate, partition manager, `SegmentSealer` and `StorageSegmentSink`. With 12,000 records, no checkpoint, and the ring starting at 2000, it was RED at the test commit with the ticket's exact message and rebuilds every key after the fix. Unit-level, not a live multi-node run]
- **`earliestRetainedOffset` is the lowest of three values:** the lowest offset this node sealed, the lowest offset its sealer still holds in flight (`EvictionListener.lowestUnsealed`, from #1297), and the ring's earliest offset. A hole between those tiers is not hidden. The read that reaches it fails `SealedRangeMissing`, which refuses the fold as `FoldFailed` with an ERROR naming the missing range. `CursorExpired`, and an empty cold read below the head, also refuse as `FoldFailed`.
- **An offset still being sealed is waited for, never skipped.** The read retries in place for about 2 s (20 × 100 ms), then reports the transient `FoldInProgress`. **The cost of that fallback:** the fold discards its partial rebuild, and the next access re-reads everything from the checkpoint.
- **Unchanged: a different node taking a partition over** reads no other node's segments. There the fold still refuses once the checkpoint lags further behind than the new owner's ring reaches. [design intent — unverified on a live takeover]
- **Not built: the ticket's ring retention hold (item 2).** #1297's ruling B designs it out: the ring hands every evicted record to the sealer, which retains it and the WAL keeps it until storage has it. This relies only on what #1297 lands. Its behaviour past the pending-seal cap is still under review there, and nothing here claims more. The checkpoint-lag metric and alert (item 3) is #1302.
