### Added (2026-10-24 — #2410: upload ingest gate: authentication and per-route limits before the body, upload bounds, concurrency caps)
- **A request is classified on its headers, before any body byte is read, on HTTP/1.1 and HTTP/3.** The listener resolves the route, applies its security policy and its limits, and answers a request that fails any of them at once. An unauthenticated 50 MiB upload with `Expect: 100-continue` is answered `401` with no `100` and its body is never read, where before the whole body was buffered up to `max_request_size` and then rejected. A refused request closes the connection (`Connection: close`) without reading the body into memory. [verified: `integrations/net/http-server` `RequestGateWireTest` and `Http3RequestGateWireTest` (real socket, real QUIC client; the refused client cannot finish sending 50 MiB), `AppHttpServerIngestGateTest` in `aether/node` over both protocols]
- **Per-route body limits.** An inline-table route in `routes.toml` takes `max_size` (`"256MB"` or bytes) and `max_duration` (`"6h"`). A declared `Content-Length` over the limit is answered `413` on the headers; a chunked body (HTTP/3: a stream with no `content-length`) is answered `413` at the byte that crosses it, and the connection is closed within a second. A route that declares no `max_size` takes `[app-http] max_request_size`, and `[app-http.upload] max_size_ceiling` (default `256MB` until streaming storage) caps what a route may declare, never past what one in-memory array holds. A `Content-Length` above 2 GiB is answered `413` on the headers, whatever the route declares. The limits are replicated with the route entry, so an ingress that does not host the route enforces them with the strictest limits any serving node declared. [verified: `RequestGateWireTest`, `Http3RequestGateWireTest`, `AppHttpServerIngestGateTest` (undeclared route, ceiling, remote route), `HttpRouteRegistryUploadSpecTest`, `HttpRoutePublisherUploadSpecTest`, `GeneratedRouteIngestLimitsTest`, `AppHttpServerIngestGateTest` (`contentLengthOverTwoGiB_*`, `defaultCeiling_*`)]
- **Upload routes (`consumes` binary or multipart) get their own bounds and caps.** `[app-http.upload]` sets `max_concurrent` (default 64) and `max_concurrent_per_principal` (default 8), past which a request is answered `429` with `Retry-After`; `idle_timeout` (default `30s`) and `min_body_rate` (default `1KB` per second after a first 30 s) answer `408` and close the connection, as does a route's `max_duration` (default 6 hours). Over HTTP/1.1 the 408 is new: the general `min_body_rate` still closes silently. [verified: `RequestGateVirtualTimeTest` (virtual time), `RequestGateWireTest`, `Http3RequestGateWireTest`, `AppHttpServerIngestGateTest`, `UploadAdmissionTest`, `UploadConfigTest`]
- **`routes.toml` inline routes refuse unknown keys.** The loader used to ignore a key it did not know, so `max_sise = "5GB"` left the route at the default limit. An unknown key is now a compile error naming it and the valid keys. `resumable` and `unclaimed_ttl` are refused as not enforced yet. An unknown key under `[app-http.upload]` refuses the configuration. [verified: `RouteConfigLoaderIngestLimitsTest`, `UploadConfigTest`]
- **Limits of this change.** Upload routes still read the whole body into memory before the slice runs; the limits bound that memory, they do not stream it. The classification's authentication is repeated on dispatch (header-only, so repeating it is harmless).
- **Multipart part limits.** An inline route takes `max_files`, `max_file_size`, `max_fields` and `max_field_size`, checked on the aggregated body (already bounded by `max_size`): a request that breaks one is answered `413` before the slice runs, on the accepting node or an ingress that does not host the route. The check reads the multipart framing only (a part's size is the bytes between its header block and the next delimiter), so it decodes nothing and copies no content, and it runs on a bounded blocking executor, not on the event loop; when the executor is full the request is answered `503` with `Retry-After`. The body is decoded once, by whoever reads it. [verified: `AppHttpServerIngestGateTest` (`maxFiles_*`, `maxFileSize_*`, `maxFields_*`, `maxFieldSize_*`, `remoteRoutesPartLimits_*`, `partLimitedMultipart_isCheckedAndDispatchedOffTheEventLoop_*`, `partLimitExecutorFull_*`, over HTTP/1.1 and HTTP/3), `UploadSpecTest`, `RouteConfigLoaderIngestLimitsTest`, `GeneratedRouteIngestLimitsTest`, `HttpRouteRegistryUploadSpecTest`, `HttpRoutePublisherUploadSpecTest`]
- **A node-wide budget of declared bytes in flight.** `[app-http.upload] max_in_flight_size` (default a quarter of the maximum heap) bounds the `Content-Length` (the route's limit when none is declared) of the uploads in flight on the node; an upload that does not fit is answered `503` with `Retry-After` on the headers, and the first refusal of an episode raises `upload-byte-budget-exhausted` (WARNING), its recovery `upload-byte-budget-restored` (INFO). The node-cap and byte-budget counts, and their episode flags, change under one lock, so a release that races a refusal cannot leave a flag set below the cap. [verified: `UploadAdmissionTest`, `UploadAdmissionRaceTest`, `AppHttpServerIngestGateTest` (`byteBudget_*`), `ClusterEventAggregatorTest`]
- **Known limit until streaming storage: the aggregation stall.** Upload bodies are still copied into one array on the event loop that carries the connection: the worst stall another connection on that loop saw was 41-53 ms, 172-190 ms and 318-434 ms for bodies of 100, 300 and 500 MiB (three uploads each, 64 sampled connections, a quiet 16-core host; the multipart route, whose part-limit check runs off the loop, stalled within 50 ms of the route with no check in every pair). The part-limit check adds none of its own.
- **An answer with status `413` is no longer rewritten to `500`.** The app listener's status table had no entry for `413`, so a `413` that a handler returned reached the client as `500`. [verified: `AppHttpServerIngestGateTest` part-limit tests, red with the entry removed]
- **Node upload cap saturation raises one cluster event.** When `[app-http.upload] max_concurrent` is reached, the first refused upload raises `upload-node-cap-saturated` (WARNING) and the first upload to finish after it raises `upload-node-cap-restored`; the event is raised once per episode, not once per refused request, and the cluster event aggregator also throttles it per node. A per-principal `429`, a `413` and a `408` raise no event. [verified: `AppHttpServerIngestGateTest` (`nodeCapSaturation_*`, `perPrincipalRefusal_*`, `sizeAndTimeRefusals_*`), `UploadAdmissionTest`, `ClusterEventAggregatorTest`]
