### Fixed (2026-09-28 — #1612: HTTP/1.1 and HTTP/3 server stop was unbounded and could not report failure)
- **The ticket's premise was false.** It said `stop()` resolved before the event loops terminated. In fact both servers already waited, through a listener on the future `shutdownGracefully()` returns, which is the termination future. The lines the ticket cited are the bind-failure branch of `onBind`, not `stop()`. [verified: integrations/net/http-server/src/test/java/org/pragmatica/http/server/HttpServerStopTest.java — the ordering and rebind tests pass against the pre-fix code]
- **`NettyHttpServer.stop()` and `Http3Server.stop()` now follow the #1610/#1614 shape.** They are a dependent chain:
  - the channel close is bounded (2 s);
  - owned groups shut down with no quiet period and are awaited, bounded caller-side (6 s);
  - the first failure is reported typed.
  
  A wedged event loop used to hang `stop()` forever; it now fails with a timeout within the bound. A failed termination used to report success. Every owned stop used to wait out the default 2 s quiet period; it now finishes in well under 1 s. The `ServerShutdown` Javadoc explains why a quiet period protects nothing for HTTP. A refused channel close is the stop's reported failure, and the groups are still terminated. Shared groups are still left running. [verified: `HttpServerStopTest` — the wedged-loop and worker-termination tests fail against the pre-fix code; the quiet-period and refused-close tests redden when the quiet period is restored or the close outcome dropped]
- **A failed bind now terminates the event loops that create made before it reports `BindFailed`**, so no loop threads outlive a failed create. [verified: `HttpServerStopTest.create_bindFailure_*` reads `isTerminated()` inside the failure callback; reporting the failure before the termination completes turns both red 5 of 5 times]
- **Callers survive the new failure outcome:**
  - `AetherNode`'s shutdown chain (now `NodeStopSequence`) logs a failed management or app-HTTP stop and continues, instead of skipping the slice invoker, the storage drain and the cluster-node stop. [verified: aether/node/src/test/java/org/pragmatica/aether/node/NodeStopSequenceTest.java — reverting to a plain `flatMap` turns it red] [unverified: wiring not pinned] that `AetherNode.stop()` delegates to `NodeStopSequence.run`.
  - Stopping a listener pair waits for both and reports the first failure, whichever listener it came from: `ListenerStops.bothStopped`, used by `ManagementServer.stop()`, the management rotation and `AppHttpContext.stopServersAsync`. [verified: aether/node/src/test/java/org/pragmatica/aether/http/ListenerStopsTest.java and `AppHttpContextStopTest` — `flatMap` or dropping h3's failure turns them red] [unverified: wiring not pinned] that `ManagementServer.stop()` calls it; no test constructs a `ManagementServer`.
  - Both certificate rotations log a failed stop and restart anyway: `ListenerStops.stoppedForRestart`. [verified: `ListenerStopsTest.stoppedForRestart_stopFails_succeedsSoTheRestartRuns` — removing the recover turns it red] [unverified: wiring not pinned] that both rotations call it.
- **Known limit, not a regression:** with every event loop wedged, the node's stop runs its listener stops one after another (management then app HTTP, each up to about 8 s, then the cluster node's net/tcp stop, up to about 8 s). That exceeds Ember's 10 s `NODE_TIMEOUT`. Before this change the same stop hung forever. [mechanism: sequential bounded stops]
