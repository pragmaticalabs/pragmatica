### Fixed (2026-10-20 — #2138: the two ends of a simultaneous QUIC dial could keep different links)
- **When two nodes dialed each other at the same moment, each end chose which of the two connections to keep from its own clocks (the connection's age and the time it last heard from the peer), so under CPU load one end could swap to the new connection while the other kept the one just closed, and the pair lost traffic on some lanes.**
- Every dial now carries a connection generation, chosen greater than any generation either end has seen for that pair, in eight extra bytes on the CONTROL stream preamble. Both ends keep the connection with the greater generation, equal generations going to the one dialed by the lower node id, and close the other. The decision reads no clock, so the two ends reach it independently and agree whatever order the connections reach them in [verified: `PeerStateArbitrationAgreementTest` over 1,600 end-pair cells, `QuicLinkRankNetworkTest`].
- The wire changes by those trailing bytes only: the `Hello` record and its tag are untouched, and a node that reads one preamble byte and ignores the rest (every earlier version) accepts the longer preamble. A dial from a node that sends no generation is arbitrated by the previous age-based rule, so a pair with such a node keeps the previous window of disagreement [verified: `QuicLinkRankWireTest`, `PeerStateLinkRankTest.attach_oneLinkUnranked_theLegacyRuleDecides`].
