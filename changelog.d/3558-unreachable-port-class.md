### Fixed (2026-11-21 — #3558: a refused redis connect on its own event loop could hang)
- **`RedisConnection.redisConnection(config)` no longer hangs when its connect is refused or times out** (#3558, the cause of the Linux CI failure in `RedisConnectionTest`, `Timeout` after 10.02 s). The connection owns its event loop, and a refused connect closes the channel on that loop, whose close path stopped the loop at once. When the caller's thread added its connect listener only after that, netty rejected the notification ("event executor terminated") and dropped it, so the connect never resolved. The owned loop is now stopped only after the connect outcome (connected, and handshaken under TLS) is recorded. Pinned by `RedisConnectionTest` with the caller held past the close path, for a refused and a never-answering connect, against a shared-loop control.
- **The redis, http-client and aether-cli tests no longer rely on a held loopback port refusing a connect** (#3558, second failure after #3319/#3321: on Linux CI `RedisConnectionTest.unreachableServerIsConnectionFailed` saw `Timeout` after 10.02 s). Each claim is now pinned deterministically: redis through a scripted client channel factory (new package-private `RedisConnection.connect`/`RedisClient.start` overloads; a refused connect and a connect that never completes, ended by the connect timeout, are each typed `ConnectionFailed`), http-client and `ClusterHttpClientDrainStateTest` through a `RefusingHttpClient` handed to `JdkHttpOperations.jdkHttpOperations(HttpClient)`, and the child-JVM `UnreachableClusterExitTest` through a `DeadEndpoint` that accepts and closes every connection unanswered.
- `UnreachablePort` and its test are deleted from `test-logging`; it had no users left.
