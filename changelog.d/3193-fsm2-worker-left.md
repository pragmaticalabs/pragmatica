### Fixed (2026-11-14 — #3193: under FSM2 a departed worker kept its directive and roster entry and never raised COMMUNITY_MEMBER_LEFT)
- **FSM2 retires a departed worker's activation directive** (#3193). The C17b cleanup, guarded on the committed DEPARTED record and skipped while the worker is joined again, now also removes a WORKER directive and the worker's departure record in the same transaction, each at its exact value, on every departed record, inferred (STALE) ones included, as the legacy engine does at `NodeRemoved` (supersedes design rule Q1 for worker directives). The record's exact-value removal is the guard, so a C17b derived before a rejoin is refused as a whole, and no leader change can leave the record behind. The governor derives its roster from the directives, so its next announcement drops the worker and `COMMUNITY_MEMBER_LEFT` is projected from that roster put. A core's directive still waits for termination authority. Removal is terminal for the node id: a falsely declared-dead worker is replaced under a fresh node id; a process admitted again under the retired id is a new admission and is directed again (#3028). [verified: `WorkerDepartureRetirementTest`, `CommunityObservabilityForgeTest` under `-Daether.deployment.engine=fsm2`]
- **A live worker whose own directive is retired halts** (#3193). A falsely declared-dead worker (inferred STALE) that is still running now sees its own activation directive removed with no replacement; it raises the CRITICAL operator warning `own-directive-retired` (subject: its node id) and halts with `EXIT_DRAINED` (exit code 2) instead of staying a member without a directive. Operator meaning: the node does not recover by itself; it returns only by joining again (restart; a restart under a fresh node id is a new admission and is directed, #3028). [verified: `OwnDirectiveRetirementTest`, `DirectiveRetiredHaltTest`, `DrainReasonTest`]
