### Changed (2026-09-30 — #1727: acknowledged lane writes on a finished stream were lost in 2 of ~480 test runs)
- **The lane-delivery tests now diagnose a loss themselves.** When acknowledged writes on a finished or retired stream are not delivered in time, `QuicLaneOwnershipTest` waits 15 s more, then reports whether they arrived late or never, which writes are missing, which end closed first, both ends' QUIC counters, and a verdict: CONNECTION-CLOSED, RECEIVER-SIDE (the acceptor's QUIC stack received the bytes and the app did not read them) or SENDER-SIDE. A CI failure therefore localizes the defect without a rerun. [verified: integrations/consensus/src/test/java/org/pragmatica/consensus/net/quic/QuicLaneOwnershipTest.java — retirement closing instead of finishing reports RECEIVER-SIDE] [unverified in-tree: the SENDER-SIDE verdict was produced by a reviewer's relay-blackhole probe, not by a committed test]
- New coverage: delivery of a burst whose stream the peer finishes survives 3–20% datagram loss in both directions; each run asserts that loss really occurred and the stream really was finished (mid-burst in most runs at 3%, usually after the burst at 20%). [verified: integrations/consensus/src/test/java/org/pragmatica/consensus/net/quic/QuicLaneFinishUnderLossTest.java]
- #1727 itself is not fixed: the loss has not been reproduced since (0 in about 440 further runs, including runs under induced CPU load and packet loss), and its cause is not located.
